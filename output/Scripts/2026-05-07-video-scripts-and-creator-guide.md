# ⚡ 7-SECOND VIDEO

**Topic:** Your own growth is stunted by your own mind — don't be afraid to take a step back to observe prior to locking back in.
**Account:** Yoobin personal brand
**Platform:** TikTok + Instagram Reels

---

## Version A — Silent Caption (Easiest to Film, Highest Impact)

**Setup:** Tripod, eye level. Clean background. You look directly at camera. No speaking required.

**Shot:**
- 0–1s: Hold direct eye contact. Slight pause. Let them lock in.
- 1–4s: Caption line 1 appears. Hold expression — not smiling, not serious. Present.
- 4–6s: Caption line 2 appears. Slow exhale or slight nod.
- 6–7s: Hold. Let it breathe.

**On-screen caption (two beats):**
> Line 1: "Your own growth is being stunted by your own mind."
> Line 2: "Step back. Observe. Then lock in."

**Caption styling:** White text, Inter Bold, center-frame. Line 2 appears with a slight delay — let line 1 land first. Line 2 can be slightly smaller or neon green for contrast.

**Instagram caption:**
```
Not a motivational quote. An actual thing I had to learn the hard way.

The problem wasn't the market. Wasn't the competition. Wasn't the timing.

It was how I was thinking about all of it.

Sometimes the most productive thing you can do is stop, observe, and then re-enter.

#buildinginpublic #founderlife #startupfounder #mindset #cofounder #entrepreneurship
```

---

## Version B — One Spoken Line + Caption

**Script (say exactly this, nothing more):**
> "Your growth isn't being held back by the market. It's being held back by how you're thinking about it."

**Delivery:** Camera close. Calm. Like you're telling a friend something you figured out at 2am. No rushing.

**On-screen caption (overlays the spoken line):**
> "Step back. Observe. Lock back in."

**Note:** The spoken line and caption should complement, not repeat each other — line says WHO is blocking you (your mind), caption says WHAT to do about it.

# 🎬 BLOGGO TALKING HEAD — "THE TRIP I CAN'T DESCRIBE"

**Hook type:** Personal founder story / vulnerability → natural product reveal
**Format:** 30–45 seconds
**What makes this different from existing scripts:** First-person, specific memory. Bloggo is revealed as the consequence of a real experience — not a product pitch.
**Destination:** `notion` → Bloggo Content Engine

---

## Scene

Close-up. Natural window light. You're looking slightly off-camera at the start — like you're remembering something — then make eye contact. No phone prop needed until the reveal line.

---

## Script

> "I have a trip from a couple years back that I still can't fully describe."
>
> [look at camera]
>
> "I was in Tokyo. Ten days. Over a thousand photos."
>
> "I cannot tell you the name of the restaurant we went to on day four."
> "I cannot tell you what neighborhood we were in on day seven."
>
> "The photos are all still there."
> [beat — 1 full second of silence]
> "The story isn't."
>
> [slight lean forward]
>
> "That trip is why I built Bloggo."
>
> "It scans your existing photos, detects your trips automatically,
> names the places, writes the story — while the details are still real."
>
> [look down at phone, back up]
>
> "If you have a trip like that sitting in your camera roll —
> that's who I made this for."
>
> "Link in bio."

---

## Delivery Notes — Line by Line

| Line | Direction |
|------|-----------|
| "I have a trip I still can't fully describe" | Off-camera, recalling. Pulls viewer in — they want to know what trip. |
| "I was in Tokyo. Ten days. Over a thousand photos." | Simple, factual. Don't color it. The specificity does the work. |
| "I cannot tell you the name of the restaurant" | Slow down. Emphasis on "cannot." This is the moment of truth. |
| "I cannot tell you what neighborhood we were in" | Same pace. Let it stack. Two "cannot tell you" lines = weight. |
| "The photos are all still there." | Exhale. Slower. The pause AFTER this line is load-bearing — don't cut it. |
| "The story isn't." | Quieter. Almost to yourself. This is the gut punch. |
| "That trip is why I built Bloggo." | Energy shift — grounded, proud, not performative. |
| "...while the details are still real." | Emphasize THIS phrase. It's the emotional core of the product. |
| "that's who I made this for." | Direct eye contact. Warm. Not salesy — inclusive. |

---

## On-Screen Text

| Timing | Text |
|--------|------|
| "I cannot tell you the name" | `"What was that restaurant called?"` (smaller, italic — like a memory caption) |
| "The photos are all still there." | `1,000+ photos.` |
| "The story isn't." | `0 words written.` |
| "That trip is why I built Bloggo." | `BLOGGO` (large, clean reveal) |
| Final line | `link in bio` |

# 💡 5 NEW TALKING HEAD IDEAS

*Mix of Bloggo and personal founder content*

---

### 1. "The Stranger Reaction" — Bloggo
**Hook:** "I showed Bloggo to a stranger and said absolutely nothing. Here's what happened."
**Angle:** Real user reaction, unscripted. No explanation from you — let the reaction speak.
**Format:** 30s. You set up the context, then describe (or show) the reaction.
**Why it works:** Third-party validation feels more honest than any product pitch.
**Destination:** `notion` → Bloggo Content Engine

---

### 2. "I Almost Didn't Build This" — Founder + Bloggo bridge
**Hook:** "There was a moment I almost killed Bloggo before it shipped."
**Angle:** The doubt moment. What made you keep going. Reveals stakes without a sales pitch.
**Format:** 45s. Vulnerability arc — problem → doubt → decision → outcome.
**Why it works:** Viewers stay for the resolution. Bloggo is the proof you made the right call.
**Destination:** `notion` → Bloggo Content Engine

---

### 3. "Building a Startup While Your Friends Are Getting Promoted" — Founder personal
**Hook:** "My friends are getting promotions, bonuses, stable hours. I'm debugging a feature at midnight."
**Angle:** The comparison trap. The choice you made. Why you'd make it again.
**Format:** 30–45s. No product. Pure founder journey.
**Why it works:** Deeply relatable to anyone who chose the hard path.
**Destination:** `notion-personal` → YOOBIN Content Engine

---

### 4. "I Travel Differently Now" — Bloggo bridge
**Hook:** "Building a travel app changed how I actually travel."
**Angle:** You notice things differently. You think about the story while it's happening. Bloggo isn't just what you built — it changed you as a user.
**Format:** 30s. Personal observation, product is the natural conclusion.
**Why it works:** Founder authenticity. You're not selling — you're sharing how the thing affected you personally.
**Destination:** `notion` → Bloggo Content Engine

---

### 5. "What Nobody Tells You About Your First Launch" — Founder personal
**Hook:** "Everyone talks about launch day like it's a celebration. Nobody told me what it actually feels like."
**Angle:** The anticlimax, the silence, the recalibration after launch. Honest founder experience.
**Format:** 30–45s. No product pitch. Pure honesty.
**Why it works:** Every founder and aspiring builder relates to this. High save and share.
**Destination:** `notion-personal` → YOOBIN Content Engine

# 📹 FILMING TIPS — TRIPOD + CAR MOUNT

## Tripod Setups

### 1. Eye Level — "Conversation" Mode
Best for: Intimate scripts, confessional angles, anything that starts with "I want to tell you something"
- Camera at nose height, angled DOWN 10–15 degrees
- You look slightly UP at lens = trust and engagement
- Sit close — arm's reach from the phone
- Use this for: "The Trip I Can't Describe" script

### 2. High Wide — "In My World" Mode
Best for: Showing your environment, day-in-the-life content, founder lifestyle
- Mount high on a shelf or tall tripod, 5–6 feet up
- Step back so your full torso and environment are visible
- You can move, gesture, walk — camera stays fixed
- Use this for: Founder content, "building something" energy

### 3. Low Angle — "Big Statement" Mode
Best for: One key line you want to land with authority
- Camera on the floor or very low, angled UP
- You look down at camera = confident, powerful
- Use sparingly — one cut to this angle on the most important line in a video
- Use this for: "I built Bloggo because of that trip" — cut to low angle on that line only

### 4. Side Profile — "Reflective" Mode
Best for: Voiceover-style content, "thinking out loud," the mindset video
- Mount 90 degrees to your left or right
- You look away from camera into the middle distance
- Works great with the 7-second mindset video — profile shot + caption only

---

## Car Mount Setups

### 1. Dashboard Facing You — Best for Talking Head Content
- Mount suction cup on dash, phone facing driver seat
- Park the car OR film from the passenger seat while someone else drives
- This is the highest-authenticity format on TikTok — viewers trust car content because it feels unscripted
- Works for: Founder journey content, quick thoughts, reaction videos

### 2. Windshield Facing Out — "In Motion" Energy
- Mount on windshield facing the road (you as a driver in the background)
- Voiceover while driving — you don't need to look at camera
- Works for: Bloggo travel content, "I'm always moving" energy, short ambient clips

### 3. Backseat Pointing Forward — Wider Angle
- Mount in the center of the backseat, facing forward
- Captures more of you + the environment
- Works for: Longer content where you're talking for 45s+, the frame feels roomier

---

## Lighting Rules (Apply to Every Setup)

| Situation | What to do |
|-----------|------------|
| Indoors, daytime | Face a window — never have window behind you |
| Indoors, evening | Ring light or softbox at 45 degrees, not directly in front |
| Outdoors | Golden hour (5–7pm) is free premium light. Overcast = free softbox |
| Car | Park facing INTO the sun, not away from it |

**One rule that beats all others:** Your face should be brighter than your background. If your background is brighter, everything else fails.

---

## Physicality Tips

- **Hold eye contact for 2 full seconds before speaking.** The viewer's brain starts engaged before a word is said.
- **Lean slightly forward on your key line.** The micro-movement signals importance — viewers feel it without knowing why.
- **Pause longer than feels comfortable.** A 1.5-second silence on screen feels like 3 seconds to the viewer — and it creates weight. Don't edit out the good pauses.
- **Hands visible = more trust.** If you can see your hands, viewers read you as open. If you're a talking head with no hands visible, it works — but hand gestures on key lines earn attention.

# ✂️ EDITING HACKS — RETENTION + PACING

*What successful creators actually do — in the order that matters most*

---

### 1. Start Mid-Sentence
Cut the first 0.3–0.5 seconds of your clip. Start as you're already speaking the first word. Remove: any "so," "um," "okay" before the hook. Every video should open mid-action or mid-sentence. Your hook starts before the viewer's brain knows the video started.

---

### 2. J-Cuts on Every Transition
The audio from your next clip starts 0.3–0.5 seconds BEFORE the video cuts.
- In CapCut: Split your next clip, extend the audio backward under the current clip
- The viewer hears you start the next sentence while the previous shot is still on screen
- Makes edits feel conversational and smooth — the hallmark of a "produced" creator

---

### 3. Text Appears ON the Word, Not Before
Caption/overlay text must land on the exact syllable it reinforces.
- 0.2 seconds early = viewer reads it before you say it → kills the rhythm
- 0.2 seconds late = viewer says it in their head before it appears → feels sluggish
- Time it frame by frame for key phrases. Worth it.

---

### 4. The 3-Second Pattern Interrupt
Between seconds 2.5 and 3.5 of every video: add something.
- A quick zoom cut (100% → 108% in 3 frames)
- A bold text pop
- A one-frame B-roll flash
- A sound effect hit

This is the first drop-off point. Instagram and TikTok algorithms measure what % of viewers leave at 3 seconds. One small interrupt significantly improves retention past that threshold.

---

### 5. The Subtle Zoom-In on Your Key Line
On the single most important line in the video: slowly zoom in (100% → 104%) over 1 second.
- Do this ONCE per video — not every key line
- The viewer's subconscious reads "this matters" without knowing why
- In CapCut: Keyframe the scale from 100% to 104% on the clip

---

### 6. End 2 Seconds Early
Cut the last 2 seconds of dead air — you lowering the camera, exhaling, saying "yeah so anyway."
Your final word should be the final frame. The video ends on your last syllable, not 2 seconds after it.
This alone improves average watch percentage significantly.

---

### 7. Captions: Bold, White, Black Shadow — Not CapCut's Templates
Auto-captions in CapCut (use the built-in feature). Then:
- Font: Bold only
- Color: White
- Shadow: Black, 60% opacity, 2px offset
- Position: Center-bottom (not bottom-edge, not top)
- Remove CapCut's default colored backgrounds on individual words — it looks like a template

80%+ of your videos are watched on mute. Captions are not optional. They are the video.

---

### Bonus — The "Nod Cut"
Between two takes of the same line (when you want to use a jump cut but soften it):
Add a 4-frame sequence of you nodding or looking down between the cuts.
Viewer reads it as a natural transition, not an edit. Small detail. Big feel.
