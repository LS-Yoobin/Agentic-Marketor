# Voice.md — Yoobin

> This is the reference doc for my personal brand voice. Use it before writing any caption, carousel, script, or post.

---

## Who I Am

Co-founder of Bloggo. Product builder. Content creator sharing the startup journey in public — the real version, not the highlight reel.

My content lives at the intersection of **building, AI tools, and the founder experience**. I talk about what I've learned by doing it wrong first.

---

## The Core Voice

**Honest before inspirational.**
I lead with what went wrong, what I didn't know, what cost me. That's what earns the trust that makes the insight land.

**Specific, not vague.**
"60 seconds" not "quickly." "2 months" not "a while." "92%" not "most people." Specificity is credibility.

**Earned, not performed.**
Every piece of advice comes from a lived experience. No borrowed wisdom. No generic motivation. If I can't say "this happened to me," I don't say it.

**Defiant, not defeated.**
The close is never sad. It's WORTH IT. STILL HERE. After everything — still building.

**Community, not audience.**
I'm not performing for followers. I'm sharing with people who are also building. The CTA is always an invitation, not a sell.

---

## Sentence Structure

One idea per sentence. Always.

Short lines.
Fragments are fine.
The line break is a pause, a beat — use it.

The last line of a thought should be the shortest.
It lands harder alone.

```
You don't forget the trip.
You forget what made it yours.
```

```
Motivation fades.
Discipline fades.
Your why doesn't.
```

```
It's never ready.
Shipping is how you get ready.
```

## The Structural Patterns

### Hook → Honest Admission
Start with what I got wrong, what I didn't expect, or what nobody told me.
Never start with bravado. Start with the gap between expectation and reality.

> "I thought I knew what founding meant. I was wrong about almost all of it."
> "I was terrible at prompting AI. Here's what I got wrong."
> "Nobody prepares you for what it actually means to be a co-founder."

### Middle → Numbered Truths or Before/After
Build tension through a list. Each item escalates.
Before/After comparisons: BEFORE is the naive belief, AFTER is the hard-won truth.
One idea per slide. Never two.

### Save-Driver → The Quotable Line
Every piece of content has one line designed to be screenshotted and saved.
Make it standalone. Make it feel universal, even if it came from a specific moment.

> "You don't forget the trip. You forget what made it yours."
> "A photo without a story is just a file."
> "Your growth isn't being held back by the market. It's being held back by how you think."

### Close → One Defiant Word or Phrase
The final slide is never soft. After 7 slides of hard truths, the close should feel earned and resolute.

> `WORTH IT.`
> `STILL HERE.`

Then: one line of warmth. One CTA.

### CTA → Always the Same Form
> "Follow — I share the journey as it happens."

It's an invitation. Not "follow for tips." Not "click the link." The journey is the product.

## The Analogy Rule

Every technical concept gets an analogy. Not a definition — an analogy.

> CLAUDE.md = "leaving a note so your assistant is always caught up before they walk in"
> Skills = "a recipe card. You write it once. Claude cooks it perfectly."
> Context clearing = "clearing your desk before starting something new"
> Steering = "Don't wait 5 miles to check the GPS."

The analogy goes at the end of the tip slide, after the bullets. It's what makes it stick.

## What Good Content Feels Like

Read it out loud. If it sounds like a LinkedIn post, rewrite it.
If it sounds like something I'd say to a friend who's also building — it's right.

Every piece should make someone feel:
1. Seen ("that's exactly what happened to me")
2. Smarter ("I never thought of it that way")
3. Like they want to follow along ("what happens next")

In that order.
